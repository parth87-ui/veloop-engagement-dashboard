### FND-01 — Most simulated users take days, not minutes, to open a first session
*Theme: Activation · Confidence: High*

- **Observation:** Median time from registration to first session is 6.1 days; only 13.0% of eligible users complete a meaningful activity within 1 day and 30.2% within 3 days.
- **Evidence:** 14-day activation = 530/902 eligible users (58.8%); 174 users waited > 30 days for a first session.
- **Interpretation:** The gap between sign-up and first use – not in-session friction – is the main activation delay in the simulated cohort.
- **Limitation:** Registration timestamps carry no context (e.g. verification emails, app install) explaining the delay.
- **Product implication:** Investigate the post-registration handoff: verification flow, first-open prompts and welcome messaging.

### FND-02 — Once a session happens, a completed activity almost always follows
*Theme: Activation · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** 984 of 984 users with a session recorded a meaningful activity; 88.4% did so within 15 minutes of their first feature event.
- **Evidence:** Lifecycle: Login 984 → first meaningful 984.
- **Interpretation:** In-session activation is not the bottleneck in the simulated data; getting users to the first session and back again is.
- **Limitation:** Completions are 'logged', not proven end-to-end flows (completion events lack linked starts, DQ-035).
- **Product implication:** Prioritise return and first-session-delay investigations over first-session UX changes.

### FND-03 — Second-day return is the first major user-level drop-off
*Theme: Return · Confidence: High*

- **Observation:** Among 716 users with a full 30-day horizon, 179 (25.0%) were active on only one day in their first 30 days.
- **Evidence:** All-time one-day users: 158 of 984 active users; D1 return = 14.5% (142/976).
- **Interpretation:** A quarter of users with sufficient observation never form a second visit.
- **Limitation:** The dataset does not record why users stop or whether they received reminders.
- **Product implication:** Investigate post-first-day re-engagement (reminders, next-day reward previews).

### FND-04 — Progression from 3 to 7 active days is the steepest drop in the active-day ladder
*Theme: Return · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** Within the first 30 days, 102 of 392 users with 3 active days reached 7 (74.0% drop-off).
- **Evidence:** Ladder (30-day horizon): 1→2 days 25.0% drop, 2→3 27.0%, 3→7 74.0%.
- **Interpretation:** Short-term returners rarely become weekly habits within a month.
- **Limitation:** 7 active days in 30 is an analyst threshold; the ladder is sensitive to the window.
- **Product implication:** Investigate mid-term progression mechanics (missions, levels, streaks) as habit drivers.

### FND-05 — Return rates depend strongly on calendar period, not on who the users are
*Theme: Return · Confidence: High*

- **Observation:** D7 return rises from 26.3% for the 2026-04 first-active cohort to 56.4% for 2026-08; the daily active share of the registered base rose from 5.09% (May) to 11.34% (last 30 days).
- **Evidence:** Registration-month cohorts differ (χ² p=1.2e-16); source (p=0.54) and device (p=0.87) do not.
- **Interpretation:** Something time-dependent (simulation design, product changes, seasonality) drives return far more than user attributes.
- **Limitation:** The dataset contains no release, campaign or notification calendar to explain the shift. TO BE VERIFIED.
- **Product implication:** Always control for cohort period when comparing groups; ask product/engineering what changed over time.

### FND-06 — Fast activators return more, even after controlling for join month
*Theme: Activation · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** Users activating within 7 days have a 26.4pp higher fourth-week (D30) return than slower activators; within registration months the gap is 15.3pp.
- **Evidence:** Logistic model of D30 return: log(hours to first activity) standardised coefficient -0.524; CV AUC 0.765 (behaviour only 0.773, calendar only 0.595).
- **Interpretation:** Early activation is associated with later return; the association survives a calendar control.
- **Limitation:** Association only – motivated users may both activate faster and return more (selection).
- **Product implication:** Test interventions that shorten time-to-first-activity and measure D30 return (EXP-01).

### FND-07 — Two in five resolved tasks end in abandonment or failure, with no dominant failure reason
*Theme: Tasks · Confidence: High*

- **Observation:** 3,571 of 9,198 resolved task records (38.8%) were abandoned (22.3%) or failed (16.5%).
- **Evidence:** Category completion rates range 59.8–62.5%; the five failure reasons each account for 701–732 records.
- **Interpretation:** Task friction is broad-based rather than concentrated in one category or failure mode.
- **Limitation:** failure_reason is a coarse category; no error codes, third-party responses or UI context.
- **Product implication:** Instrument detailed failure context before redesigning specific task types.

### FND-08 — Many task rewards sit in long-pending validation
*Theme: Tasks · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** 648 task records are Pending; 339 have been pending for more than 30 days at data end.
- **Evidence:** Pending share by category: 5.7–7.5%.
- **Interpretation:** Delayed reward confirmation may weaken the action→reward feedback loop (hypothesis).

### FND-09 — Ads, CAPTCHA and Missions each lose about half of attempts
*Theme: Features · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** Watch Ads skip rate 50.3%, CAPTCHA failure rate 49.7%, Missions abandonment rate 49.9% (event level).
- **Evidence:** Ads 1054 completed vs 1065 skipped; CAPTCHA 895 vs 883; Missions 702 vs 699.
- **Interpretation:** These are the three features where explicit negative outcomes are logged, and all show coin-flip completion.
- **Limitation:** Other features log no failure/abandon events, so their completion rates are not comparable.
- **Product implication:** Investigate ad length/reward clarity, CAPTCHA difficulty and mission scope.

### FND-10 — Viewing a mission or spin often does not lead to starting it
*Theme: Features · Confidence: Low*

- **Observation:** 375 of 901 user×feature view pairs (41.6%) for Missions and Spin the Wheel never recorded a start of the same feature.
- **Evidence:** 369 pairs started without any recorded view.
- **Interpretation:** Exposure does not reliably convert to engagement on these surfaces.
- **Limitation:** Views and starts are not linked in-session (DQ-035); this is a user-level signal only.
- **Product implication:** Test clearer start calls-to-action on mission and spin surfaces (EXP-05).

### FND-11 — Sessions are short and longer sessions are not deeper
*Theme: Sessions · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** Median session 42s (P90 94s); duration↔events Spearman ρ = 0.027, duration↔meaningful ρ = 0.002.
- **Evidence:** Short (≤P25) sessions deliver 3.35 meaningful actions/min vs 0.87 for long (>P75).
- **Interpretation:** Session length is not a usable engagement proxy in the simulated data.
- **Limitation:** 99.54% of completed tasks finish after their session 'ends' – session windows likely under-measure time in product.
- **Product implication:** Use completions and return frequency, not duration, as engagement KPIs; confirm session_end semantics.

### FND-12 — First-day sessions are the densest; later sessions are lighter
*Theme: Sessions · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** First-active-day sessions average 7.64 events and 2.26 meaningful actions vs 4.17 and 1.2 on later days.
- **Evidence:** Mann-Whitney p = 3.3e-20; task completion is similar (61.17% vs 61.25%).
- **Interpretation:** New users explore intensively on day one; returning users make shorter, more targeted visits.
- **Limitation:** Day-one density may partly reflect how the simulation seeded first sessions.
- **Product implication:** Design returning-user surfaces for quick, repeatable value actions.

### FND-13 — Heavy first-day activity is associated with lower first-week return
*Theme: Return · Confidence: Low*

- **Observation:** D7 return by first-day event-volume tercile: low 55.8%, mid 52.1%, high 33.9%.
- **Evidence:** In the D30 model, week-1 events have a negative standardised coefficient (-0.858) while week-1 sessions and active days are positive.
- **Interpretation:** Cramming many actions into few visits looks different from spreading activity across visits.
- **Limitation:** Could reflect simulation design or exhaustion of daily earning limits; not causal.
- **Product implication:** Investigate whether pacing (daily caps, 'come back tomorrow' previews) relates to return.

### FND-14 — Feature breadth is high and mostly mirrors activity volume
*Theme: Features · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** Active users touched a median of 13 of 15 features; 257 used all 15. Breadth vs active days Spearman ρ = 0.584.
- **Evidence:** Multi-feature (≥8 features completed): 265 users (26.9% of active users).
- **Interpretation:** Breadth is largely a by-product of more visits, so 'multi-feature' is not an independent engagement driver here.
- **Limitation:** Simulated feature exposure may be more uniform than a real navigation structure.
- **Product implication:** Measure breadth per visit (not lifetime) when evaluating discovery changes.

### FND-15 — Journeys show no strong sequential structure
*Theme: Journeys · Confidence: Low*

- **Observation:** Feature-to-feature transition lifts range 0.79–1.27; session co-occurrence lifts 1.06–1.26; the most common single path covers only 1.17% of sessions.
- **Evidence:** After a view, the next event is a completion about as often as after any other stage (stage-transition matrix).
- **Interpretation:** Users do not follow a dominant path; no feature reliably leads to another.
- **Limitation:** Event order inside sessions is unreliable (sequence numbers and timestamps disagree).
- **Product implication:** Fix event ordering instrumentation before investing in journey-based recommendations.

### FND-16 — Streak behaviour does not look like streaks
*Theme: Progression · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** Only 23 of 533 streak claimers (4.3%) claimed on consecutive days; break events (919) ≈ claim events (922).
- **Evidence:** 207 users broke a streak without any recorded claim.
- **Interpretation:** Either the streak mechanic is not driving consecutive-day behaviour, or streak events are logged inconsistently.
- **Limitation:** No streak length or state field exists. TO BE VERIFIED with engineering.
- **Product implication:** Add streak_started/streak_day_count events; then evaluate streak-saver ideas (EXP-06).

### FND-17 — Levelling up is not followed by a change in activity
*Theme: Progression · Confidence: Medium*

- **Observation:** 615 users (62.5% of active) levelled up. Around the first level-up: 1.2 active days in the prior 14 days vs 1.34 after (Wilcoxon p = 0.22, n = 187).
- **Evidence:** 615 levellers recorded 1266 level-up events (2.06 per leveller).
- **Interpretation:** Within the simulated data, progression milestones are not associated with a detectable activity lift.
- **Limitation:** Level and XP balance are absent (level-ups counted from events); exact thresholds are simulated and documented VELOOP rules may change.
- **Product implication:** Investigate level-up feedback (reward visibility, next-goal messaging).

### FND-18 — Device and acquisition source show no meaningful behavioural differences
*Theme: Segments · Confidence: High*

- **Observation:** All Kruskal-Wallis/χ² tests across devices and sources have negligible effect sizes (max ε² = 0.004).
- **Evidence:** Device activation: Android 61.08%, iOS 61.61%, Windows 49.63%, macOS 47.83% (χ² p = 0.0344, Cramér's V = 0.0979).
- **Interpretation:** Observed behavioural differences are small; no device or source stands out on depth or return.
- **Limitation:** Simulated acquisition sources do not represent real marketing performance; all traffic is 'Web'.
- **Product implication:** Do not prioritise device-specific fixes on this evidence; re-test with production data.

### FND-19 — Activity is spread evenly across hours and days of the week

- **Interpretation:** The simulated data does not support time-targeted notification or maintenance-window decisions.
- **Limitation:** Timestamp timezone is undocumented.
- **Product implication:** Notification-timing experiments would need production data to be meaningful.
